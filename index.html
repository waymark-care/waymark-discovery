Rigor

How a finding earns a place on this page

Findings reach this page by one of two routes. Waymark's published papers have passed journal peer review, and we feature only the non-intuitive results a Waymark clinician has approved. Agent hypotheses face five statistical gates instead: continuous testing produces false positives unless the error rate is controlled across the whole stream. LORD++ controls the false discovery rate at 5% across the open-ended stream under independence; tests that share members are dependent, so treat this as approximate. Each candidate is then confirmed on a held-out half of members and checked against a negative-control outcome that should show nothing.

Papers from the literature
  1. Screened for relevance to the programs Waymark runs.
  2. Identity checked against the registry record (DOI, PubMed or arXiv), with retracted papers removed.
  3. Appraised for study design, then marked as applies or watch.
  4. Corroborated against other studies, and challenged by a skeptic agent.
  5. Translated into a local test where Waymark's data can answer it, and run through the same gates as any other hypothesis.
  6. Approved by a Waymark clinician before it appears here.

Member data never leaves Waymark

Discovery runs inside Waymark's secure environment. What reaches this page is a short whitelist of ratios, ids and labels, checked by software twice and by a clinician once.

Findings are associations in Waymark's de-identified operational data. They generate hypotheses for care design and are not causal estimates. Effect sizes are rate ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Literature cards summarize the cited study; read the source for its full methods and limits.

False discovery rate control with LORD++ assumes independent tests. Hypotheses that share members are dependent, so the stated rate is approximate.
